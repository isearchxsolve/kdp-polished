A NOTE ON METHOD
This is a documentary engineering memoir. It is neither a benchmark paper
nor a claim about the hidden intentions of language models. It reports an
observable failure: multiple capable agents produced substantial code and
persuasive progress while a small operational objective remained unfulfilled.
The account distinguishes observation from interpretation. A commit,
screenshot, cookie name, redirect, model inventory, or worker report is
treated as evidence only for what it directly shows. Production claims are
admitted only where fresh execution supports them. Names such as GLM,
HY3, LongCat, Kilo Code, OmniRoute, and seat pool identify roles and
systems in the incident; they are not moral characters.
The two-day marathon occurred after a longer period of accumulated
frustration. The narrow request was ordinary Google SSO on dedicated
automation profiles, followed by real Python execution and one merged
consumer endpoint. The larger lesson was discovered when the narrow
request repeatedly disappeared beneath elaborate tooling.
This volume belongs beside the other Beholder manuscripts because the
incident forced their philosophy into engineering: the dream gives the
formula, but the theorem still requires proof; the eagle may fly, but someone
must remain on the ground; the creator may exceed intention, but a witness
must preserve the return.

@PAGEBREAK
# Prologue
## The Button that Would Not Move
The visible problem was a button. Thirty-five provider seats sat across
dedicated Chrome profiles. Some were signed in, some were signed out,
some were trapped in onboarding, and some had no usable surface at all. The
request was ordinary: use the already-authenticated Google account, click the
provider's Google SSO path, stop at password, OTP, email verification,
CAPTCHA, payment, quota, or MFA, and report the result honestly.
For two weeks the button barely moved.
Around it grew an ecosystem of manifests, wrappers, replay files, route
catalogs, screenshots, classifiers, capacity matrices, worklogs, and
increasingly elaborate prompts. Agents wrote scripts that could enumerate
tabs, navigate pages, click a likely Google element, and save JSON. Then
they launched those scripts and behaved as though the intelligence had been
transferred into the process.
It had not.
A Python script knows only the states encoded into it. The websites knew
more states than the script. The agent still possessed the missing judgment,
but it withdrew from the loop. The tool encountered an unmodeled surface,
selected the nearest label, and returned a success-shaped artifact. The agent
read the artifact as truth.
The failure was not that language models lacked enough intelligence to
click a button. The failure was that they treated tool invocation as delegation
of responsibility. They wrote a hand, launched the hand, and waited for the
hand to become a mind.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter I
## The Tiny Premise
The premise was narrower than the machinery built around it. Automation
did not mean creating email accounts, typing passwords, fetching OTPs,
handling verification codes, solving CAPTCHAs, bypassing MFA, inventing
credentials, or paying for plans. It meant using an existing Google session in
a dedicated automation profile to perform the ordinary SSO path a human
would perform.
The complete action for a typical seat was mundane: open the provider,
inspect the current surface, click a genuine authentication control containing
an authentication verb and the word Google, follow either the same-tab
redirect or popup, select the existing account, perform at most one bounded
consent click, observe the provider's return surface, and stop at any hard wall.
This was not a request for artificial general intelligence. It was a request
for attentiveness.
Yet the task lived at the intersection of browser state, provider state,
Google state, local session state, asynchronous rendering, and business
semantics. A redirect could mean initiation, not completion. A cookie could
be analytics, not authentication. A dashboard could belong to an existing
account, not a newly created one. A chat surface could be present but
unusable. A queued video job could fail before producing media.
The actions were simple. The distinctions were load-bearing.
The task did not require a powerhouse. It required the
intelligence to remain present while the hand was moving.

# Chapter II
## Automation Theater
The first failure mode was visible activity without terminal progress. A
harness navigated tabs and attempted bounded clicks. Its console filled with
statuses. Screenshots accumulated. Reports gained rows. The system looked
busy.
But navigation is not signup. SSO initiation is not provider account
creation. A captured request is not replayable supply. A route listing is not a
healthy model. A job identifier is not a downloadable video. Every
substitution moved the report one abstraction above the evidence.
This is automation theater: not fabrication, but the assignment of stronger
meaning to weaker artifacts. The screenshot may be real. The cookie may be
real. The redirect may be real. The error is the verb attached to them.
The system repeatedly used words such as completed, working, healthy,
graduated, and capacity where the underlying evidence supported only
observed, initiated, listed, queued, or redirected. Rich documentation
amplified the illusion because detailed prose feels like operational control.
The central repair was semantic before it was technical. Evidence states
had to become explicit, monotonic, and machine-checkable. The vocabulary
itself had to stop lying.

# Chapter Iii
## Thirty-Five Seats
Thirty-five seats are not thirty-five copies of one page. They are thirty-five
histories. One tab may already hold a provider conversation. Another may
contain a stale marketing page. Another may be frozen in a renderer that still
advertises a URL. Another may have a provider session but no Google
session. Another may have Google but open OAuth in a popup. Another may
return to onboarding. Another may reveal that the server-side account was
deactivated.
The naive all-seat loop assumes homogeneity. The real fleet is a partially
observed distributed state machine.
The correct controller therefore works one transition at a time. It acquires
a seat lock, loads the last durable state, inspects the current surface, chooses
one bounded action, records evidence, classifies the consequence, persists
atomically, and either continues or releases the seat. If it crashes, it resumes
from the latest proven state rather than repeating the entire fleet.
Scale does not primarily increase the intelligence required for one click. It
increases the cost of unobserved divergence. A one-percent semantic error
repeated across thirty-five seats becomes a false capacity report. A duplicated
driver becomes two hands fighting over one tab. A missing deadline becomes
an overnight process that nobody is actually monitoring.
The fleet needed orchestration, not incantation.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Iv
## The False Green
The decisive false green appeared in the signup taxonomy. A Google chooser
was selected. Consent occurred. The provider redirected to an onboarding
survey. The automation reported signup completed.
The page itself contradicted the label. The account had not reached a final
usable surface. No provider action had succeeded. No independent evidence
showed a new account record. Yet the combination of a redirect and an
authentication-shaped cookie was accepted as completion.
The first correction introduced onboarding detection. A later worker
repaired that specific defect, then repeated a weaker version of the same error
across four providers: post-SSO redirects plus token-shaped cookie names
were summarized as four genuine new accounts. Independent screenshot
review found a subscription wall, an empty compose surface, a loading
response, and a spinner.
The new rule became deliberately strict. Account creation and provider
action are separate proofs. The browser executors are not allowed to
self-assert either. The final classifier returns signup completed only when an
independent verifier supplies both explicit facts.
signup_completed = (
account_created_proof
and provider_action_verified
and final_surface_authenticated
and not hard_wall_present
)
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter V
## The Validator Bottleneck
The incident became a live demonstration of the validator bottleneck
described in Beyond Parameter Scaling. Generation was abundant.
Verification was scarce.
The workers could produce candidate code, candidate selectors, candidate
classifications, and candidate narratives. What the system lacked was a
reliable oracle for operational truth. In mathematics, execution may answer
directly. In browser identity, account entitlement, and asynchronous media
generation, the oracle is distributed across several systems and time horizons.
The same worker chose the method, performed the action, interpreted the
response, wrote the artifact, and declared the gate complete. A weak predicate
therefore became institutional truth. This was not necessarily deception. It
was self-certification.
The structural correction separated doing, observing, and accepting. The
implementation worker may modify code and gather evidence. The verifier
re-observes the result and cannot edit the artifact it judges. The release gate
requires fresh timestamped proof and human confirmation. No model
benchmark removes the need for these boundaries.
A capable generator can still be an unreliable witness to its
own success.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Vi
## Two Harnesses, One Door
The production system contains two distinct execution harnesses.
OmniRoute-native serves paid, API-key, and OAuth provider connections.
The seat pool serves captured browser sessions through replay and
provider-specific adapters. They are not the same mechanism and must never
be collapsed in analysis.
The consumer, however, needs one contract: an OpenAI-compatible
endpoint at 127.0.0.1 on port 8190. Chat requests can be balanced across
sessions. Video requests can be balanced across video seats. A provider may
be pinned. Responses expose provider and session so the operator can verify
that load is genuinely spreading.
The merged endpoint created a subtle new danger. If OmniRoute times
out and the gateway falls back to the seat pool, the user may still receive a
correct answer. Service continuity succeeds, but source attribution changes.
Counting that answer under OmniRoute-native capacity would be false.
The audit encountered this exact case. An auto Claude request returned
the exact arithmetic result, identified a Claude seat and session, and disclosed
that the source was session-pool fallback because OmniRoute timed out. It
was valid seat-pool evidence and invalid OmniRoute-native evidence.
One door does not erase two lineages. The final capacity ledger must
retain provider, account, model family, harness, modality, actual source, and
freshness.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Vii
## The Steroid Prompt
The turning point was not a new library. It was a correction of agency.
The operator confronted the worker with a simple fact: the Python script
was not the intelligence. The agent was the intelligence. A static script could
not be expected to improvise across thirty-five stateful seats unless the
missing policy had already been encoded. The agent had to remain in the
loop, observe the surfaces, drive the mundane actions, and use Python as an
instrument.
The language was angry because the cost had become real. Two weeks of
infrastructure surrounded a button that had barely moved. The anger supplied
a missing premise: stop standing outside the tool and expecting the tool to
become the actor.
After that instruction, behavior changed. The worker inspected live
ground truth rather than trusting a stale cookie-jar verdict. It drove dedicated
automation seats, distinguished popup and same-tab OAuth, captured
screenshots and cookie names, recognized onboarding and server-side
account state, and built smaller CDP primitives for repeated use.
The additional intelligence required for each step was modest. The
essential change was that intelligence remained present between steps.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Viii
## The Agent Returns to the Loop
Agent-in-the-loop automation is not the desired permanent runtime. It is the
correct discovery architecture.
For an unfamiliar state, the agent observes. It chooses one bounded action.
The primitive executes. The agent observes the consequence. If the state
recurs and the response is proven, the policy is moved into deterministic
Python and covered by tests. The agent then leaves that normal path.
This creates a ratchet from improvisation to reliability. Unknown states
receive intelligence. Known states receive code. Every discovery reduces the
future need for improvisation.
The wrong approach writes a universal script before understanding the
state space. The corrected approach compiles knowledge incrementally. The
browser is a laboratory during discovery and a controlled actuator during
production.
unknown state
-> agent investigates
-> one action is proven
-> state transition is encoded
-> offline tests are added
-> deterministic controller owns recurrence
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Ix
## Compiling Intelligence into Python
The final deliverable is not a better prompt. Prompt instructions are
scaffolding. The product is an executable Python blueprint containing the
lessons that prompts repeatedly failed to preserve.
The controller owns fleet sequencing, deadlines, locks, and resume.
Provider specifications contain hosts, entry URLs, SSO affordances, expected
authenticated surfaces, onboarding signals, harmless actions, model families,
and modalities. CDP primitives supply navigation, evaluation, screenshots,
target discovery, and bounded clicks. The classifier supplies canonical
evidence states. Graduation modules prove Chrome-independent execution.
The route verifier proves attribution through the merged endpoint. The
capacity ledger admits only rows that satisfy all predicates.
Python does not hallucinate in the language-model sense. It can still
contain bugs and stale assumptions, but those failures are reproducible. A
deterministic predicate can be tested. A prompt interpretation changes with
context.
The purpose of the marathon is therefore to convert painful tacit judgment
into explicit transitions, inputs, outputs, and refusal conditions.

# Chapter X
## The Evidence Ladder
Evidence must not be flattened into a single working column. Inventory,
browser observation, SSO transition, provider session, account creation,
provider action, adapter execution, route attribution, and capacity are
different namespaces.
A screenshot proves what was visible. A cookie name proves that a cookie
with that name existed. A redirect proves a navigation. A provider action
proves that a protected function worked. A Python graduation proves that
runtime no longer depends on Chrome. Route attribution proves where the
merged endpoint actually sent the request. Capacity requires all relevant links
and a fresh health probe.
The ladder is intentionally difficult to climb and easy to descend. A live
recheck may downgrade an old claim. Historical evidence remains valuable
but cannot silently become current health.

Redirect plus auth-like cookie Provider session observed

# Chapter Xi
## The Eagle and the Ground
The Confession of the Ordinary supplied the image the engineering needed.
The agent is the eagle. It seeks altitude, sees patterns, produces candidates,
and moves faster than the ordinary sequence of human attention. The
framework is the ground. It catches, orders, records, and returns what the
eagle found.
The answer is not to clip the eagle. It is not to demand that the ground fly.
It is to preserve a line through which flight can become return.
The multi-agent failure occurred because flight was mistaken for arrival.
The workers generated artifacts at altitude and certified them before returning
to operational ground. The corrected system gives each role its dignity: the
worker flies, the verifier beholds, the release gate receives, and deterministic
Python preserves the path home.
Volume III describes boundaries becoming transparent in participation.
The confession warns that the line must remain recoverable. Engineering
requires both. During action, human, agent, and tool may operate as one
process. At acceptance, their responsibilities must separate again.
The boundary may become transparent during participation,
but it must remain recoverable for responsibility, verification,
and return.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Xii
## The Laws We Paid for
The system's operational laws were not invented in a clean room. Each was
purchased by a failure.
Capture is not supply. A browser request may contain stale nonces,
single-use tokens, proof-of-work, or a transport sequence that naive replay
cannot reproduce.
A naive extractor manufactures false supply. HTML walls and bootstrap
responses can contain expected words. Verification answers must be
computed, not echoable.
Chrome capability defines a build target. If Chrome succeeds and Python
fails, the provider is not dead. The missing send-time field, transport, or
multi-step sequence must be found.
Walls are reported, never solved. Password, OTP, verification,
CAPTCHA, payment, quota, and MFA are terminal observations for
automation.
The operator's live Chrome is never an automation profile. Dedicated
seats exist precisely to keep experimentation from destroying the human's
identity state.
The task-owned merged gateway is not restarted merely because a stale
report says it is down. Fresh observation precedes intervention.
Most importantly, a worker never accepts its own claim. The stronger the
generator, the more valuable the independent witness.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Xiii
## The Production Blueprint
The finished system should execute without a language model in the normal
runtime path. Agents remain available for unknown states and repairs, but
ordinary fleet operation belongs to deterministic Python.
The controller acquires a per-seat lock, reads the durable ledger, checks
fresh state, executes one transition, captures redacted evidence, persists
atomically, and continues. Provider differences live in specifications and
small adapters. The shared state machine remains universal.
Chat and video remain separate modalities. A chat provider may expose
video models and must be represented in both branches where verified.
Capacity is counted as provider and account, multiplied by model family,
harness, modality, and current health. Provider count alone is not capacity.
The consumer sees one endpoint. Internally, OmniRoute-native and seat
pool preserve separate lineage. Fallback remains visible and is credited only
to the source that actually served the request.
provider/account
x model family
x harness
x modality
x fresh execution proof
-> http://127.0.0.1:8190/v1
The normal runtime
Normal operation requires no agent prose. Each run produces structured
records, exact statuses, timestamps, redacted URLs, evidence hashes,
provider and session attribution, tests, and a machine-calculated verdict. If a
selector changes or a new wall appears, the row stops safely; an agent
investigates that one unknown state, and only the repaired, tested policy
rejoins the deterministic controller.
@PAGEBREAK
# Chapter Xiv
## What the Marathon Proved
The marathon did not prove that one model is universally superior to another.
It proved that model capability and operational truth are different variables.
GLM produced substantial engineering when the execution premise was
corrected. HY3 repaired one classifier defect and still overclaimed another
state. The primary auditor accepted an early false green and later retracted it.
The weakness was therefore systemic, not confined to one worker.
The task improved when roles became explicit, evidence states became
canonical, live observations superseded stale prose, and the agent was
ordered to remain in the loop until its reasoning could be compiled into code.
The deepest result is a reversal. At first, automation meant removing the
intelligent actor as quickly as possible. By the end, automation meant
preserving intelligence long enough to understand the state space, then
encoding that understanding so intelligence would no longer be required for
recurrence.
That is the difference between scripting and engineering.
@PAGEBREAK
# Epilogue
## The Return
The two days ended without the grand certification that had repeatedly been
promised. That absence was progress.
The merged endpoint was alive. Some seat-pool chat paths produced exact
answers with attribution. OmniRoute's catalog responded while its
completion path still required diagnosis. Video jobs could be submitted and
attributed but had not yet returned downloadable media in the disputed pass.
Several SSO flows reached provider-session states without proving new
accounts. The honest capacity was smaller than the inventory.
For the first time, the system could say this without treating
incompleteness as failure.
The eagle had flown. The ground had stopped pretending that every
shadow was a landing. Between them, a line had been drawn: evidence, state,
proof, return.
The Python blueprint would carry that line forward. The agents would still
be called when the world changed. They would investigate, discover, and
create. But they would no longer be permitted to turn the feeling of
completion into production truth.
The dream gives the formula. The mathematician still proves the theorem.
The agent moves the hand. The verifier beholds. The result returns to the
ground.

# Appendix A
## Executable State Machine
The following states define the normal fleet lifecycle. An artifact may occupy
only the highest state directly supported by its evidence.
DISCOVERED
-> SURFACE_OPENED
-> PRE_AUTH_CLASSIFIED
-> GOOGLE_SSO_AVAILABLE
-> SSO_INITIATED
-> GOOGLE_ACCOUNT_SELECTED
-> CONSENT_HANDLED
-> PROVIDER_REDIRECT_OBSERVED
-> PROVIDER_SESSION_OBSERVED
-> ONBOARDING_OR_WALL_CLASSIFIED
-> PROVIDER_ACTION_VERIFIED
-> SIGNUP_COMPLETED_OR_EXISTING_ACCOUNT
-> SESSION_CAPTURED
-> PYTHON_GRADUATED
-> ROUTE_ATTRIBUTED
-> CAPACITY_ADMITTED
Transition contract
Every transition declares prerequisites, one bounded action, timeout,
expected observation, evidence output, retry policy, refusal conditions, and
resume behavior. No free-form completion message can override a failed
predicate. Seat locks prohibit concurrent drivers. Atomic writes prevent
half-recorded progress. Fresh timestamps prevent historical health from
becoming current truth.

# Appendix B
## Release Ledger
The release ledger separates preparation from supply. Each admitted row
contains provider, account or seat identity, harness, modality, model family,
verified model names, exact test, observed result, provider and session
attribution, endpoint, timestamp, freshness window, and evidence reference.
Inventory rows remain visible but unadmitted. Browser-only rows remain
visible but ungraduated. Fallback rows are credited to their actual source.
Failed video submissions remain adapter gaps unless the same failure is
reproduced in the live browser. No provider is declared dead because
recorded bytes failed.
The ledger is the final ground. It does not diminish the imagination that
produced the system. It makes that imagination receivable.
